Your Questions Answered

We understand you have questions. Here are honest answers to what we hear most often.

Ask Us Your Question

An uncontested divorce typically takes 4–6 months from the date of filing. A contested divorce — where spouses disagree on children, property, or support — can take 1–3 years depending on complexity and court scheduling. We work to resolve your matter as efficiently as possible without sacrificing your entitlements.

In Canada, the most common ground for divorce is one year of separation. You and your spouse must have lived "separate and apart" for 12 consecutive months — though you can live under the same roof if you are clearly living separately. You can begin the divorce process before the year is up, but the divorce order will not be granted until the one-year period is complete.

Ontario uses an "equalization of net family property" model. Each spouse calculates their Net Family Property (NFP) — the value of property accumulated during the marriage. The spouse with the higher NFP pays the other spouse half the difference. There are exclusions, including gifts, inheritances, and property owned before the marriage. The matrimonial home is treated differently and is always subject to equalization regardless of when it was acquired.

Yes. If you and your spouse agree on all issues, your lawyers can negotiate a Separation Agreement that resolves everything out of court. A judge still needs to grant the Divorce Order, but neither of you needs to appear in person. This is the fastest and least expensive path. We offer mediation support and uncontested divorce representation for clients who qualify.

Financial disclosure is mandatory in Ontario family law proceedings. If your spouse fails to disclose or you suspect they are hiding assets, we can bring a motion for full financial disclosure, subpoena financial records, engage forensic accountants, and ask the court to draw an adverse inference — which can actually increase your equalization payment. We have extensive experience uncovering hidden assets in high-conflict divorces.

Costs vary significantly. An uncontested, cooperative divorce can cost between $2,500–$5,000 in legal fees. A contested divorce involving disputes over assets, support, and custody can range from $15,000 to well over $100,000 in complex cases. We are transparent about our fees from day one and offer flexible billing arrangements. Your free consultation includes a fee estimate based on your specific situation.

The matrimonial home has special status in Ontario — both spouses have equal possession rights, regardless of whose name is on the title. The home can be sold and proceeds split, or one spouse can "buy out" the other. Courts will always prioritize stability for any children involved. We help you understand your rights to the home and negotiate the best outcome for your specific situation.
